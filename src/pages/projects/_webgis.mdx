## iCAT-Shiny: Spatial Analysis in Cancer Catchment Area

### Overview
An interactive platform for analyzing spatial data related to cancer incidence and catchment areas. This tool enables researchers to assess cancer impacts within specific geographic locations, enhancing targeted healthcare resource allocation and intervention planning.

### Objectives
- Support data-driven decisions in healthcare
- Visualize cancer-related data spatially
- Analyze patterns across defined catchment areas
- Benefit public health professionals and researchers

## JCOIN Dashboard: Justice Community Opioid Innovation Network

### Overview
Part of the NIH HEAL Initiative, this project addresses the opioid crisis by providing a spatial view of opioid use disorder (OUD) resources across justice-involved populations. The dashboard maps federal grants to states and cities, enabling comprehensive analysis of OUD interventions.

### Key Features
- Interactive mapping of OUD resources
- Federal grant distribution visualization
- Spatial analysis tools for policy development
- Integration of justice and healthcare data

### Impact
The JCOIN Dashboard serves as a crucial tool for:
- Policy makers tracking intervention effectiveness
- Researchers studying OUD patterns
- Practitioners coordinating resources
- Government agencies allocating funding

## Selected VUE Geovisualization Projects

### Mapping the Chicago Riot of 1919

**Principal Investigator:** John J. Clegg, Harper Schmidt Fellow and Affiliate faculty in the Dept. of Sociology

This project visualizes the spatiotemporal evolution of the 1919 Chicago riots on the city's South Side. Utilizing newspaper clips, photographs, and other archival digital data, the visualization showcases the progression of four primary classes of incidents: injury, arson, bombing, and homicide. The project maps the gradual spread of these events, allowing users to view the historical data overlaid on both contemporary 1919 maps and current street maps.

*Note: This work was featured in a UChicago News article and serves as a highlighted digital resource for the Chicago 1919 project. It also accompanied the "Loose Machinery" symposium organized by Prof. Clegg and Prof. Eve L. Ewing.*

**Expertise:** Spatiotemporal Visualization, Historical Data Mapping, WebGIS

**Project Links:**

### Historical Homicide Geography
#### Exploring the prevalence and geography of homicide in three cities from 1923 to present

**Principal Investigator:** Robert Vargas, Neubauer Family Assistant Professor

This research focuses on the spatiotemporal analysis and visualization of historical homicide location hotspots. The project explores the underlying causes of change in the prevalence and geographic distribution of homicides across three major cities—Chicago, New Orleans, and San Francisco—spanning from 1923 to the modern era. By integrating historical homicide datasets with historic city maps, detailed GIS hotspot analysis is utilized to reveal long-term spatial patterns in violent crime over time.

**Expertise:** GIS Hotspot Analysis, Spatiotemporal Analysis, Historical Cartography

**Project Links:**
* [UChicago Justice Project](http://justiceproject.uchicago.edu/)

---

### Business of Debt

**Principal Investigator:** Destin Jenkins, Neubauer Family Assistant Professor of History

The Business of Debt project complements and expands upon the book *The Bonds of Inequality: Debt and the Making of the American City*. Developed in collaboration with the RCC, this project features a fully interactive map visualization utilizing six decades of data (1931–1981) from bond buyers' directories. The maps display the physical locations of the nation's bond trading firms across all available cities and states. Starting with addresses for Chicago, New York, and San Francisco in 1931, the platform visualizes the evolving geographic network of financial institutions and their branch affiliates over a fifty-year time horizon.

**Expertise:** Interactive Map Visualization, Network Visualization, Spatial Data Integration

**Project Links:**
* [Destin Jenkins Research & Digital Humanities](https://www.destinjenkins.com/research)
* [VUE Project Documentation](https://vue.rcc.uchicago.edu/node/12)
